### 02_corpus_composition.png

Left: class counts in the cross-validation pool, from the committed fold predictions. Stage 4/5 (n=89 across the corpus) and AP-ROP are the classes flagged 'not externally validated'. Right: the three disjoint analysis sets, log scale — note the image-to-infant ratio differs sharply by set.

### 04_screening_score_shift.png

Source: results/rop/preds_weights_test.npz and screening_external_spec.npz. The threshold 0.1933 was fitted to the left panel. In the right panel the LOWEST score of any image is 0.279 — above the line — so all 663 images, including all 150 healthy eyes, are flagged. Specificity 0.000. This is not a bad threshold; it is a threshold applied to a distribution it was not fitted to.

### 05_roc_transfer.png

Both models scored on the SAME 663 external images, so the gap is a property of the models, not of the test. The structured model loses discrimination away from home (0.970 -> 0.821) — every model does — but it retains far more of it than the head it replaced (0.691, approaching the 0.5 of a coin toss).

### 06_threshold_sweep.png

The selection rule was fixed before the curves were drawn: take the LARGEST threshold whose sensitivity stays at or above 0.90 on both sites. Largest, because specificity is what is being bought. Both sites, because satisfying only the training hospital is exactly the failure in figure 04.

### 07_rebase_impact.png

Sensitivity is traded DOWN (0.990 -> 0.958 internally) to buy specificity that did not exist outside the training hospital at all (0.000 -> 0.387). Caveat carried from the artifact: the external dev half was used to SELECT the threshold, so its two green bars are in-sample for that choice and optimistic.

### 08_ppv_vs_prevalence.png

Sensitivity and specificity are properties of the model and survive a change of population. PPV does not. At the 13% prevalence of a real NICU, most positives from this tool are false — which is why the product words a positive as 'have a clinician look' and never as a diagnosis.

### 09_calibration.png

Bins with fewer than 10 images are dropped. The app labels this score 'calibration unverified' and asks the reader to treat it as a ranking, not a probability of disease; this figure is the evidence for that wording. Read the orange curve with care: the held-out set is 77% ROP-positive against 43% internally, so it sits above the diagonal partly because of PREVALENCE and not only because of miscalibration. The honest summary is that a score means different things at different sites, which is the same lesson as figure 04 measured a different way.

### 10_evaluation_unit.png

Source: results/rop/locked_eval/locked_table.json (n=661 images, 230 eyes, 116 infants). Bars are the mean over 5 checkpoints, error bars their SD. Nothing about the model changes between the red and green bars — only the unit the label actually describes. This gap, not an architecture, is the paper's finding, and it holds in all four arm-seed cells.

### 11_camera_view_misses.png

The ROP ridge grows in the retinal PERIPHERY. Temporal frames look at it and are missed 12.7% of the time; posterior-pole frames look away from it and are missed 71.7% of the time. The errors have an anatomical direction, which is what distinguishes a labelling-unit artefact from a model that simply cannot see disease.

### 12_confusion_locked.png

5 checkpoints pooled (3,305 predictions over 661 images). The dominant error is the NORMAL COLUMN, not the diagonal's neighbours: 73% of stage-1 frames, 44% of stage-2 and 37% of stage-3 are called Normal, while the Normal row itself is 99% correct. That asymmetry IS the label-unit effect — a frame of a diseased eye that does not contain the ridge is, as a picture, normal — and it is why figure 10's eye-level score is so much higher. Stage 4/5 (n=4) and AP-ROP (n=12) rows are too small to read as performance.

### 13_per_class_locked.png

Mean over 5 checkpoints. Stage 4/5 and AP-ROP carry so few locked images that their bars are noise, and the paper marks them 'not externally validated' rather than quoting them — a pre-registered decision, not one taken after seeing these values.

### 14_head_equivalence.png

Both arms use identical folds, sampling and preprocessing — only the head differs. The mean difference is −0.0055 at seed 42 and +0.0224 at seed 1337: OPPOSITE SIGNS. A difference that flips direction when only the random seed changes is noise. Reporting either seed alone would have produced a confident and wrong claim, and an earlier version of this project did exactly that.

### 15_site_probe.png

Reported as a NEGATIVE result. At every adversary strength the disease-controlled probe stays near 0.86-0.92 — far above chance — so the branch did not remove site appearance. The naive probe sits LOWER than the controlled one, which is the trap: an uncontrolled probe understates the leakage it is meant to detect.

### 16_session_unit_decomposition.png

Sources: results/rop/ablation_session_level.json (natural-sampling arm) and session_level_by_seed.json. The familiar headline — AP-ROP recall rising from image level to session level — conflates TWO effects. Reweighting: one Ostrava infant supplies 76% of AP-ROP images, so weighting units equally moves the number before any aggregation happens. Aggregation: taking the max over a session's frames moves it again. Right panel: on fold-0 val, aggregation dominates; on the pooled 5-fold split recorded in the same artifact, reweighting dominates (~70%). PINNED for the paper: the pooled split is primary — it covers every internal AP-ROP session exactly once, where fold-0 val holds 21 sessions and its split depends on which fold drew the dominant infant. Fold-0 is shown as the instability, not the estimate. Session recall itself replicates across seeds: 0.840 (100/119) at seed 42 and 0.832 (99/119) at seed 1337.

### 17_cam_attention_audit.png

Sources: results/rop/cam_leakage.json (n=663, both models) and cam_objective_control.json (n=120). LEFT: the served model letterboxes, so 25% of its frame is black bars added by preprocessing — pixels that are black by construction and carry no information. MIDDLE: because the two models' frames devote different areas to non-retina, only area-normalised lift compares them; both sit below 1.0, i.e. both prefer retina over frame. RIGHT, and the reason this figure exists: swapping the scalar that Grad-CAM differentiates moves total off-retina attention by 26.3 percentage points on identical weights and identical images. Off-retina attention is therefore NOT a model-intrinsic property, and the raw cross-model comparison we first ran (40% vs 8%) was measuring letterboxing and objective choice, not shortcut reliance. The metric keeps the job it was built for — flagging one map whose evidence sits off the retina — and loses the job it was never valid for.

### 18_cam_matched_objective.png

Sources: results/rop/cam_matched_objective.json (n=663, 2 models x 2 objective forms) and cam_peak_location.json. Figure 17 showed the first cross-model comparison was invalid — different objectives, different frames. This is the rerun that makes it valid: both models measured under the same objective FORM, twice. The gap survives both matchings (34 points under log-prob, 19 under raw logit), so the served structured model genuinely parks its Grad-CAM peak outside the retina far more often than the head it replaced. RIGHT, the honest magnitude: the camera-surround share — the only zone both frames contain — is stable across objective forms (12% and 11%), while the padding share is what the objective swings (24% vs 11%). The like-for-like claim is therefore ~12% vs 2-3%, not 36% vs 2%: the structured model's peak lands on the camera's own border about four times as often as the retired head's, and the rest of the raw gap was letterbox geometry. Lift stays below 1.0 for every cell (0.65-0.69 vs 0.31-0.35): both models weight retina above uniform; the structured one does so far more weakly.
