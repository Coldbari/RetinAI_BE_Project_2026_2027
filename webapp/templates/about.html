Model Card & Methodology

A transparent record of the data, training and validation behind this ROP screening assistant — a clinical decision-support prototype that assists, and does not replace, ophthalmologists. This deployment screens for one disease: Retinopathy of Prematurity.

What the system does

  • Grades a neonatal fundus photograph into six ICROP classes (Normal, Stage 1, 2, 3, Stage 4/5, AP-ROP) and, from the same output, screens it for any ROP.
  • One model answers both questions: an ensemble of five DINOv2 ViT-S/14 networks, whose probabilities are averaged.
  • Shows a Grad-CAM heatmap of what moved the any-ROP score, with the share of attention that fell outside the retina, and generates a downloadable PDF report.
  • Grades a whole eye from 1–16 photographs by its most severe view.
  • A gradability gate rejects non-retinal input, and patient context is mandatory: this app has no adult models, and it says so instead of guessing.

Important limitations

  • Research prototype, not a medical device; no prospective clinical validation.

Preprocessing & serving

  • Circle-crop to the retinal field of view, letterbox to a square without changing the aspect ratio, then CLAHE contrast equalisation on the LAB luminance channel; resize to 392 px and normalise with ImageNet statistics.
  • The same preprocessing module runs in training and serving. Training read images that went through it once and were stored at 768 px; the app runs it on the original upload.
  • No test-time augmentation: each of the five networks sees the image once.
  • Gradability gate: redness, saturation, blur, luminance and field-of-view checks calibrated on training data only, plus a feature-space check against real fundus photographs.
  • Uploads below ~1500 px get a resolution warning: an earlier model lost measurable sensitivity on downscaled input.
