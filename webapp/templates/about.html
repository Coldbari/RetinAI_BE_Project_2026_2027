Model Card & Methodology

A transparent record of the data, training and validation behind this ROP screening assistant — a clinical decision-support prototype that assists, and does not replace, ophthalmologists. This deployment screens for one disease: Retinopathy of Prematurity.

What the system does

  • Screens a neonatal fundus photograph for ROP (No ROP / ROP) at a sensitivity-first operating point.
  • Returns a prediction, a model score (calibration measured: ECE 0.041 through the served 5-view TTA path), risk level and recommendation.
  • Shows a Grad-CAM heatmap (where the model looked) and generates a downloadable PDF report.
  • Includes a 6-class ICROP staging research preview (Normal, Stage 1, 2, 3, Stage 4/5, AP-ROP) — clearly labelled, not a clinical output.
  • A gradability gate rejects non-retinal input, and patient context is mandatory: this app has no adult models, and it says so instead of guessing.

Important limitations

  • Research prototype — not a regulated diagnostic device; no prospective clinical validation.
  • The binary model's pooled AUC of 0.927 is partly camera recognition: with capture device held constant it is 0.881, and every ROP-positive test image came from one camera. The device-controlled figure is the honest one.
  • Per-infant specificity at the deployed threshold is 0.000 — no healthy infant in the test cohort was fully cleared. This is a triage filter, not a rule-out test.
  • The staging preview is a research model: cross-validated but single-seed, its locked external test has deliberately not been opened, and Stage 4/5 rests on 89 training images. Do not read its output as a clinical grade.
  • Earlier versions of this app also screened DR and glaucoma. Both were retired from the product — glaucoma after our own source-confound audit made its pooled number undefendable, DR because it is not the current focus. We only expose what we currently stand behind.

The staging model's evaluation produced two findings we consider more useful than the model itself: AP-ROP labels are per-examination-session, so image-level recall (0.345) understates true detection (0.840 per session, CI 0.76–0.90, at 0.25% false alarms on normal sessions); and the domain-adversarial site branch that looks like it removes hospital information under a naive probe leaves a disease-controlled probe unmoved at every strength we tried. Both are written up in the project repository.

Preprocessing & serving — identical to training

  • Circle-crop to the retinal field of view, then CLAHE contrast equalisation on the LAB luminance channel.
  • 5-view test-time augmentation (original, flips, ±10°), averaged — the calibration and threshold above are measured through this exact path.
  • The same preprocessing module runs in training and serving, so what was measured is what is deployed.
  • Gradability gate: redness, saturation, blur, luminance and field-of-view checks calibrated on training data only; false-rejection measured at 1.7% on 4,000 held-out adult fundus images.
  • Uploads below ~1500 px get a resolution warning: preprocessing applies a fixed-radius filter before resizing, and a measured experiment showed downscaled input costs real sensitivity.
